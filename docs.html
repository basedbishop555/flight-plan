Flight Plan is an unofficial, open-source helper for sAERO voters on Aero. It reads the chain, suggests a split sized to your locks, shows how you rank, and rehearses the vote for free. It never asks you to sign anything. You vote in the Aero app.

How to use it

  1. Paste your wallet address (or connect a wallet, which only fills the address in). Flight Plan finds your sAERO locks on chain.
  2. Read your split. The big number is what this window is expected to pay. Tap any pool for the detail behind it.
  3. Dry run. A free rehearsal on the chain from your address: it shows exactly what your votes would become. Nothing is signed or sent.
  4. Copy the split and vote those percentages in the Aero app.

How a window works

Each sAERO lock votes for pools. After a vote the lock waits 47 hours; then its window opens and it can move again. A vote keeps earning until you change it, so there's no rush the moment a window opens.

Locks earn two things from the pools they vote for: fees (a share of trading fees) and streams (incentives paid to voters for a set time). Both are split among everyone voting for that pool, so a well-paid but crowded pool can pay less per AERO than a quiet one.

How the split is made

A scanner reads every Aero pool on Base every hour and estimates what each pays voters over a 47-hour window:

  • Fees from a blend of the last 12 hours, 24 hours, 2 days and 4 days, with spikes capped and fading pools trusted down. Weekends count at 70% (tokenized stocks at 5%, their markets close).
  • Streams for the hours they overlap the window. A stream ending within 8 hours of the open is ignored.
  • Crowding. Voters pile into the pools that look best, so each pool is priced for a pile-on, measured after every window.
  • Your own size. Your AERO dilutes the pool too: your share = your AERO ÷ (the pool's votes × pile-on + your AERO). The pool's votes are read live when you open the page, with your own current votes taken out.

Then it spreads 100% in 5% steps, each step going where it adds the most expected dollars, under these rules: 6 to 10 pools · at most 25% in one pool · at most 40% in one pool type · at least a third in fee-led pools · your slice at most 10% of any pool · small pools (under 250k votes) at most 10% each · no tokenized stocks when 30% or more of the window is a weekend · any pool whose votes doubled since the last scan is skipped.

"Expected" is the middle case; the low case assumes a heavier pile-on. APR = the window's expected dollars on your AERO, annualized. The split is valued for a window starting at the later of now and your first lock's open.

Picking how many pools

Auto uses the rules above (6 to 10 pools). Pick 1 to 10 for exactly that many: each pool may then take up to 100 ÷ N percent (rounded up to 5%), and with 3 or fewer the per-type cap is dropped. The 10%-of-a-pool cap always stays, so a big wallet asking for one pool gets a "pick more pools" note instead of a bad bet. Fewer pools can pay more, and can also be hit harder if the crowd follows you in.

Why your split differs from someone else's

The split is sized to your locks. Your own AERO dilutes the pool and the caps scale with your size.

Example: a small pool has 200,000 AERO voting for it and will pay voters $100 this window.

A 1,000 AERO lockputs 5% here (50 AERO): 50 ÷ 200,050 of $100, about $0.025. Per AERO that's the pool's full rate, so small wallets happily use small, well-paid pools.
A 2,000,000 AERO walletcan put at most 10% of the pool here (20,000 AERO), 1% of the wallet: 20,000 ÷ 220,000 of $100, about $9.09. The rest has to go somewhere deeper.

Rank: your locks vs everyone

  • All your locks count as one: combined dollars over combined AERO, for now (the last 12 hours), 2 days and 7 days.
  • Every lock / Every wallet. Every lock compares you with each sAERO lock on its own. Every wallet groups all of an address's locks into one entry (a contract such as a vault counts as one wallet).
  • Size filter (10k+ to 1M+) ranks you among locks or wallets at least that big. It starts on your own size.
  • Whole-lock APR. Earnings are divided by everything a lock holds, including AERO parked and not voting anywhere, because parked AERO earns nothing. When that differs from the APR on the voting part alone, the voting APR shows small underneath.
  • Average and median are the plain average and middle APR of every lock (or wallet) over 100 AERO in the filter, each counted once.

Earnings are read on chain for every lock (fees and incentives, claims bridged) and valued at current prices. Rank data refreshes every 3 hours.

What it reads, and never does

  • Reads only: your locks, their votes, pool sizes, and a dry run (eth_simulateV1, a simulation that changes nothing) through a public Base RPC, straight from your browser.
  • Connect asks your wallet for its address and nothing else. There is no Sign or Send button anywhere.
  • Never: an approval, a transfer, a claim, or any transaction. Nothing you enter is sent to a server; the page is static files plus public chain reads.
If any site claiming to be Flight Plan asks you to sign or approve something, it isn't this one. Close it.

Never sign blind (when you vote in the Aero app)

  1. Dry run here first so you know exactly what your votes should become.
  2. Network is Base, the To address is the Voter below, and the value is 0 ETH.
  3. Your wallet's preview should show your sAERO lock re-allocated and no tokens moving.
Reject anything that says "setApprovalForAll", "approve" on sAERO, or "transfer", or a value other than 0. Voting moves votes, never tokens.

Hardware wallets show the vote as raw data, so the To address, network and 0 ETH are the checks you can make on the device.

Locks voting on other networks

Aero runs on several networks (OP, Arbitrum, Ethereum, Ink, Robinhood, Arc) and a lock can vote on any of them. Flight Plan plans and rehearses Base votes. If a lock's votes sit on another network, its card says so and the dry run is skipped; you can still use the suggested percentages for it in the Aero app.

Data and freshness

Pool estimates are published every hour; ranks every 3 hours. How many votes each pool has is read live when you open the page. The footer shows how old the pool data is, and the page warns when it's more than 3 hours old.
